📚 How Raising Cane's Labor Tracking Works

The Big Picture

Labor tracking at Raising Cane's is built around one core question: "Are we spending the right number of crew hours for the number of customers we served?"

The answer comes from comparing Actual Adjusted Crew Hours against the Allowable Hours from the Labor Matrix.

Step 1 — Get Customer Counts

Pull from Aloha Configuration → Sales Daily Summary (pages 4–5). These are your actual transaction counts for the day. Customer counts are bucketed in 50s.

Step 2 — Get Raw Labor Hours

Pull total hours from Aloha. This includes everyone who clocked in — managers, crew, trainees, etc.

Step 3 — Adjust Hours

Raw hours aren't your "real" crew hours. You need to add and subtract several things:

ADD (hours that happened but aren't in system):
  • Crew who forgot to clock in
  • Hours worked from home or off-site
  • HR-corrected hours
  • New hire tablet/training pay not in POS

REMOVE (hours that are in system but shouldn't count toward crew labor):
  • Manager hours (at least 1 manager required every shift, up to 3 per day)
  • Manager in Training (MIT) hours
  • Crew in Training hours
  • Crew who forgot to clock out (ghost hours)
  • Pre-shift meeting ("Meet") hours

Step 4 — Read the Matrix

Your restaurant has an Hours Band (e.g. 14.0) assigned per day in Hours of Operations. The Labor Matrix is a lookup table:
Customers → round down to nearest 50 → find row → find column for your band → that's your allowable hours
Key rules:
  • Allowable hours don't increase below 400 customers (flat at base)
  • Increases kick in at 450+ customers
  • Every 50 customers adds a specific number of hours depending on your band

Step 5 — Calculate Variance

Variance = Adjusted Crew Hours − Allowable Hours
Negative variance = GOOD — you're spending fewer hours than allowed.
Positive variance = OVER LABOR — you're spending more hours than your traffic justifies.

A common practice: target −5 to −10 hours going into close to account for closers staying late. Closers add buffer, and you want that buffer to keep you at or below 0 at the end of the night.

Step 6 — Make Decisions

Based on variance and projected customers:
  • Large positive variance (overspend): Cut crew hours — send people home early, cut a shift
  • Near zero or slightly negative: Hold current — watch the next hour
  • Large negative (under budget): Consider calling someone in if it's getting busy

Shift handovers happen ~3 times daily: Lunch, Dinner, Late Night. Each handover is when you reconcile hours and plan cuts/calls.

Variance % Target

You're allowed up to −5% variance after close. Profit-forward periods make this especially useful — you can bank negative variance as a competitive advantage.

Tools Used

  • Aloha Configuration — source of truth for customer counts and raw labor hours
  • HotSchedules — projected customer counts and scheduled crew hours
  • Deployment Workbook — the Period/Week workbook where you enter daily hours + customers
